The Kid-Song Cheat Sheet

Everything you need to get a good children's song out of Suno on the first or second try — the exact slider numbers, a fill-in-the-blank style box, and how to write words a five-year-old can sing back. Made to be worked through by a grown-up and a kid together.

Start here

Four steps, every single song

Do them in this order. Most bad songs come from skipping straight to "press Create and hope."

STEP 1

Pick one idea

One song teaches one thing. Sharing. Counting to five. Putting toys away. Not all three.

STEP 2

Write the chorus first

The chorus is the part kids remember. Write it before anything else, then build verses that lead into it.

STEP 3

Fill the style box

Use the recipe below. Describe the sound — never name a real show or singer.

STEP 4

Set sliders, then create

Weirdness low, Style high. Listen to both takes all the way through before deciding.

Advanced options

The three sliders

Open Advanced Options in Custom mode to find these. Kids' songs want the opposite of what a cool adult song wants: predictable, clean, and singable. That means low Weirdness and high Style.

Weirdness15
SafeChaos

How much Suno is allowed to surprise you.

Keep it low. A surprise key change or a sudden rap verse ruins a sing-along. If something shows up that you never asked for, drop this to 5–10.

Style Influence80
LooseStrong

How hard Suno obeys your style box.

Keep it high. This is what stops your ukulele song from turning into a pop record. If the song comes out sounding like grown-up radio music, raise this — do not raise Weirdness.

Audio Influence85
FreeLocked

Only appears when you upload or cover audio.

Ignore it unless you're reusing a backing track — like putting new words on your show's theme music. Then set it high to keep the original music, or drop to 75 if the singing sounds stiff.

The one rule that saves the most time: change one slider at a time. If you move two and it gets better, you've learned nothing about which one did it — and next week you'll be guessing all over again.
Style box

The fill-in-the-blank recipe

Seven parts, separated by the | character, always in this order. Suno pays most attention to what comes first, so the kind of song goes at the front and the "don'ts" go at the end.

The template
what kind of song, for young children | how fast + how it feels | 3–5 instruments | who is singing | the mood | how it should be recorded | what to avoid

Three that are ready to paste. Change the instruments and you have a whole new song.

Learning / counting song
cheerful children's counting song | bright and bouncy, around 100 BPM, easy to clap along to | ukulele, glockenspiel, upright piano, hand claps, shaker | warm friendly grown-up voice with a small children's chorus answering back | happy, playful, patient | clean and simple, every single word easy to hear | no rock guitar, no heavy drums, no synth bass, no sad chords
Silly high-energy song
silly children's dance song | fast and goofy, around 128 BPM, stompy and bouncy | tuba, kazoo, marching snare, cowbell, tambourine, honky-tonk piano | excited grown-up voice with a whole classroom of kids shouting back | funny, wild, joyful | live and a little messy like a school band, everything up front | no serious singing, no ballad, no sad chords, no long held notes
Calm goodbye / naptime song
gentle children's lullaby | slow and soft, around 70 BPM, gently rocking | nylon string guitar, soft glockenspiel, brushed drums, warm upright bass | one calm grown-up voice singing close and quiet | sleepy, safe, kind | very simple, lots of space, nothing loud or sudden | no big drums, no horns, no key change, no crescendo
Never name a real show, singer, or character. Suno blocks it, and even when it slips through it doesn't work. "Sounds like a famous puppet show" gets you nothing; "ukulele, glockenspiel, hand claps, warm grown-up voice with kids answering" gets you the actual sound you were picturing.
Lyrics

Five rules for words kids can sing

01

Short lines — four to seven words

A kid sings back what fits in one breath. Long flowing lines physically can't be delivered like short punchy ones, so the length of the line controls the whole feel of the song. This does more work than anything you write in the style box.

02

Put the lesson in the chorus

Whatever you want them to remember goes in the part that repeats. If the lesson only shows up in verse two, it isn't in the song.

03

Repeat the chorus almost word-for-word

Change one word at most between choruses. Getting creative on the repeat is the fastest way to make a song nobody can sing along to.

04

Leave a hole for them to answer

Call and response is the whole trick of a kids' show. Write the answer in parentheses — Suno reads it as a second voice coming back at you.

05

Use blank lines to make it breathe

Empty lines tell Suno to leave space. Without them it crams everything into one rushed breath. Two lines, blank line, two lines is a reliable shape.

Section tags go on their own line, in square brackets. These are the ones worth using:

[Instrumental Intro] [Verse 1] [Chorus] [Verse 2] [Bridge] [Instrumental Break] [Outro] [Kids' Choir]

You can also give one section its own direction: [Chorus: kids shout the numbers back]. That changes only that section, without touching the style box.

A complete example — swap the objects for your own
[Instrumental Intro]

[Verse 1]
I found a shiny button on the floor
Then I found another by the door

Help me pick them up and count with me
Counting is more fun when there are three!

[Chorus]
One! (One!)
Two! (Two!)
Three! (Three!)
Four! (Four!)
Five little buttons on the floor!

One, two, three, four, five —
Now let's count them one more time!

[Verse 2]
I found a yellow pencil on my chair
Then I found another over there

Hold them up and count them nice and slow
Are you ready? Here we go!

[Chorus]
One! (One!)
Two! (Two!)
Three! (Three!)
Four! (Four!)
Five little pencils on the floor!

One, two, three, four, five —
Now let's count them one more time!

[Instrumental Break]

[Outro]
Five! We found five!
Great counting, everybody!
Two traps worth knowing before they bite you. Anything you write in the intro can come back and repeat all song long — so only put your hook up there, never a one-time line like "here we go." And if the words start too early, put [Instrumental Intro] at the very top with nothing written under it.
The voice

Give Suno a situation, not adjectives

This is the single biggest thing we learned, and it transfers straight over to children's music. Adjectives produce a generic voice. A situation produces a performance, because Suno has something to act.

Weak — describes a textureStrong — describes a situation
warm, friendly, kid-friendly voice singing to twenty five-year-olds sitting on a carpet three feet away, not performing on a stage
excited, energetic delivery getting a whole room of kids up off the floor and moving
soft, gentle, soothing singing quietly to one child who is nearly asleep
Then lock it with a Persona. As soon as one take genuinely sounds like your show's host, save it as a Persona and use it on every song after that. Written descriptions drift a little every time; Personas hold. If the show has more than one character who sings, build one Persona per character — and build each from the most energetic take you have, so that calmer songs read as the character holding back.
Troubleshooting

When it comes out wrong

Find the symptom, make one change, generate again.

What happenedWhat to change
Sounds like a grown-up pop songRaise Style → 85. Not Weirdness.
Something appeared you never asked for — a rap, a sad breakdown, a key changeDrop Weirdness → 5–10
Technically correct but lifeless and stiffStyle 75 / Weirdness 25
Can't understand the wordsShorten your lines. Add clear diction, every word easy to understand to the style box.
Singer sounds too grown-up or too breathyReplace the adjectives with a situation (see above)
Words start before the music gets goingEmpty [Instrumental Intro] at the top
One line keeps coming back over and overIt's in the intro block. Move it, or make it the hook on purpose.
Your uploaded backing track driftedRaise Audio → 95
Singing sounds trapped on the wrong notesLower Audio → 75
Song is too long / loses themCut a verse. Two verses and three choruses is plenty.
Working together

Who does what

The kid

Creative director
  • Picks what the song is about
  • Writes the chorus — the part everybody sings
  • Decides which of the two takes is better, and says why
  • Names the song
  • Tests it: can you sing it back after hearing it twice? If not, it isn't done.

The grown-up

Engineer
  • Fills the style box and sets the sliders
  • Changes only one thing between tries
  • Downloads the keeper before making any changes to it
  • Saves and manages the Personas
  • Keeps a note of which style box made each good song

Both

Every time
  • Listen to both takes all the way to the end before choosing
  • Be willing to throw one out — the next generation is thirty seconds away
  • Say out loud what was wrong with it before changing anything
Last look

Before you press Create

Slider numbers are starting points, not laws — they come from a lot of generations and they'll get better once you keep your own notes. The habit that matters more than any number on this page: write down the style box that made a song you loved. In a month you will not remember it.
